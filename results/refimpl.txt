CRASH CONSISTENCY OF THE CAPSULE COMMIT
    10/10 checks passed
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

A1  Power cut before every operation of the commit
  ok  A1 power cut before each of 6 operations, x4 epochs x 16 survival subsets
        -> 384 coherent
        224 rolled back to the previous epoch, 160 rolled forward to the new one; no
        other outcome occurred. The subsets cover reordering: any pending write may or
        may not have reached the medium
  ok  A1 both outcomes actually occur
        -> both
        a protocol that only ever rolled back would pass the invariants and lose every
        update

A2  Torn writes at the boundaries that matter
  ok  A2 torn writes at 8 boundaries x 6 operations
        -> 96 coherent
        including a record complete but for its last digest byte
  ok  A2 a record with a valid digest over a truncated body
        -> rejected
        the magic and the digest agree; the length fields do not

A3  Which syncs are load-bearing, by ablation
  ok  A3 sync 1 of 3 removed, on the new slot record
        -> load-bearing
        204 invariant failures without it. First: I1 counter names epoch 2; 0 slots
        hold it
  ok  A3 sync 2 of 3 removed, on the counter
        -> load-bearing
        170 invariant failures without it. First: I1 counter names epoch 1; 0 slots
        hold it
  ok  A3 sync 3 of 3 removed, on the erase of the old slot
        -> no effect in this sweep
        removing it breaks nothing under any cut or survival subset, so it is
        redundant for correctness -- it still bounds how much work a restart has to
        redo
  ok  A3 at least one sync is load-bearing
        -> yes
        2 of 3 syncs, when removed, break an invariant under some cut. The others are
        not doing correctness work, and saying so is the point of the ablation

A4  A real process killed while writing real files
  ok  A4 real process killed at each of 6 operations
        -> 12 coherent
        12 of 12 children died before returning. Real files, real os.write and
        os.fsync, os._exit at the fault point
  ok  A4 the real runs match what the simulation predicted
        -> 12 agree
        the simulated counterpart of a killed process is the cut in which every
        pending write survives, since the kernel flushes its page cache regardless of
        what happened to the writer. Agreement is what licenses reading A1-A3, which
        are simulated, as statements about the algorithm

  What the medium is assumed to provide, and this does not check:
    - a write reported complete is durable, or is reported not complete
    - writes to distinct files are not reordered across an intervening sync
    - a torn write may corrupt a record but must not corrupt a different record
    - the counter's two records do not share a failure domain with each other

  sum of the parts             1.523 s
  measured end-to-end          1.988 s
  quorum response time             4 h   (1/rho, Section sec:results)
  mean restoration time           23 h   = 82,800 s
  the exchange as a fraction of it         2.40e-05   (4.6 orders of magnitude below)

  These are CPython `pow` on a 2048-bit modulus and are therefore a loose upper bound --
  a tuned library would be roughly an order of magnitude faster and hardware more again.
  The looseness runs in the safe direction: even at this bound the whole cryptographic
  exchange is 4.6 orders of magnitude below the quorum-gathering time that dominates the
  23-hour mean, so no plausible change to these operations moves any quantitative claim
  in Section sec:results. The converse is why it is worth measuring at all: had they
  come out in hours, the CTMC parameterisation would need redoing.
